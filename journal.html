The Journal

Life on the land.

Notes from the valley by Reid Manchester.

May 11, 2025 · 1 min read

Spring, fireflies, swimming

This happened today. The beginnings of the outdoor living room. Should go quickly from this point. The joists are always a pain, and this old pole barn is clearly not square or level in any way, so the hard part is behind us. Thanks to resident Matt who put in a little labor when my back was worn out. Soon it will have a grill, sink, dining table and chairs, sofa and enclosed with clear polycarbonate.

Soon the fireflies will fill the valley at dusk each day.

May 3, 2025 · 1 min read

Telescope and a living room?

Some years ago I bought my parents a pretty nice telescope. They don't use it. Shocker. They're donating it back to us and reclaiming space in their condo. Life in the valley is well protected from light pollution, so we're going to have viewings of stars and planets and moons!

The other night I was hanging out with Hayes and he pointed out that we don't have a spot to enjoy the land in the shade. It is something that's been on my mind for a year... nowhere to do yoga in the shade, nowhere to keep the grill dry... so we're going to build a big covered living space. The grill, a sofa, sink, table and chairs and enough space to do yoga facing the pasture.

I need another unfinished project like I need a hole in my head, but it will add so much to our lives there. Hold tight!

May 3, 2025 · 2 min read

Movie Night!

Admittedly, it was a long road getting to the dream; a place that has a sense of community. People who support one another, who are helping one another achieve goals, and have some fun.

With the arrival of Brian Carroll, projects started to accelerate. A second set of hands, a smart mind, and a caring soul really set the place to grow. We started adding a second bath to the house, and built an entire apartment inside the barn. He got the old riding mower working after I was ready to strap TNT to it and watch it go up in smoke. We rented a trencher and ran water all the way to the RV pole and beyond, connecting the well to tenants down there. That was huge, and filling in the trench we did by hand (I look jacked now). We also had a load of gravel delivered to make the road more passable when the rains come. Another load will be needed.

Then Tiffany arrived with her tiny house on wheels and brought a real taste of community. Brian helped her install hurricane straps to ensure the strong winds through the valley don't tip her over (yes, that's a real risk!). She brought us tea and drinks while we slaved on the barn apartment. And most recently she organized a BBQ and movie night. Brian assembled the community grill and we grilled with our friends and guests followed by a movie projected on the barn wall!

Now that we're moving into summer we will see the Hipcamp campers down by the creek, which often brings new friendships like my buddy Hayes, who has become a favorite friend of mine. The creek water is the right temperature for a dip now, just don't drink it!
